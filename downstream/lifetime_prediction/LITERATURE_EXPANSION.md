# Literature Expansion for Organolithium Lifetime Work

This note focuses on finding new papers that are more useful for downstream intermediate stability/lifetime modeling, rather than simply increasing total paper count.

## Priority Keywords

Use these keyword combinations first when searching:

- `"organolithium" AND "flow microreactor" AND "short-lived"`
- `"organolithium" AND "flash chemistry"`
- `"organolithium" AND "residence time"`
- `"organolithium" AND "unstable intermediate"`
- `"organolithium" AND "halogen-lithium exchange" AND flow`
- `"organolithium" AND "decomposition" AND flow`
- `"organolithium" AND "milliseconds" OR "subsecond"`
- `"organolithium" AND "trapping" AND flow`
- `"functionalized organolithium" AND flow`
- `"organodilithium" AND flow microreactor`
- `"lithium enolate" AND flow AND residence time`
- `"batch vs flow" AND organolithium`
- `"rapid mixing" AND organolithium`
- `"microreactor" AND "short-lived reactive intermediates" AND lithium`

## Best Expansion Targets

Prioritize papers with one or more of these characteristics:

- Explicit residence-time optimization
- Temperature window comparisons
- Statements like "short-lived", "unstable", "must be trapped immediately", "subsecond", "milliseconds"
- Batch versus flow comparisons
- Intermediate generation and in situ trapping
- Organolithium subclasses beyond aryllithium, such as alkyllithium, enolates, dilithiums, oxiranyllithiums

## How To Use These

- Download the high-priority primary papers first.
- Use the reviews as citation-mining entry points.
- Favor papers that expose time-scale, temperature, solvent, or trapping-window information.
- For downstream lifetime work, a smaller set of high-information papers is more valuable than many generic organolithium synthesis papers.
